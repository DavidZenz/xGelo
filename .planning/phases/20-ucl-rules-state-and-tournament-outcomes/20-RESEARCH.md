## User Constraints (from CONTEXT.md)

### Locked Decisions

#### Rules authority and tie resolution
- **D-01:** Recompute the league-phase table from accepted match state using one editioned, manually reviewed UEFA rules contract. Provider standings remain reconciliation evidence and cannot replace the project-owned ranking computation. — **Reversibility:** costly — published rank and qualification artifacts depend on the ruleset identity and trace schema.
- **D-02:** Apply every available tie-break criterion in official order and emit an auditable criterion/subset trace. If required late-criterion evidence is unavailable, stop at the last provable criterion, preserve a shared unresolved rank interval, and suppress any downstream path that requires an exact rank.
- **D-03:** Qualification bands are derived from the recomputed table: ranks 1–8 advance directly, 9–24 enter the knockout play-offs, and 25–36 are eliminated. No provider-supplied band or rank silently overrides the rules adapter.

#### League schedule and forecast ledger
- **D-04:** Accept the league-phase schedule only when all 36 clubs and exactly 144 fixtures validate with stable club and fixture identities, eight distinct opponents per club, matchday, home/away side, kickoff, lifecycle, score semantics, and source lineage. Never infer missing pairings.
- **D-05:** A fixture forecast is durable evidence only when generated by the selector-authorized club release before kickoff with a strict exclusive cutoff. Completed fixtures retain that immutable pre-kickoff forecast; they are never regenerated after the result is known.
- **D-06:** Retain one typed ledger row for every fixture. Ineligible or blocked forecasts carry an explicit reason such as release unavailable, kickoff unconfirmed, identity unresolved, insufficient model evidence, or cutoff violation; absent forecasts are not silently omitted.

#### League-phase probability outputs
- **D-07:** Condition simulations on accepted completed results and simulate only eligible remaining fixtures. Settled matches are fixed and are never resampled.
- **D-08:** Publish mutually exclusive top-8, ranks 9–24 play-off, and ranks 25–36 eliminated probabilities for every club, together with the full rank distribution and documented rank-8/rank-24 cut-line distributions.
- **D-09:** If an unresolved tie can change a qualification band and no official criterion can resolve it, the affected probability output remains explicitly unresolved instead of using lexical, source-order, or random fallback ranking.

#### Knockout path conditioning and match resolution
- **D-10:** Before an official draw is accepted, enumerate or sample only paths allowed by the editioned rank-constrained draw rules. Once a validated draw artifact exists, condition on it exactly. Never assume a fixed bracket or pair teams uniformly across illegal paths. — **Reversibility:** costly — draw-path identities flow into every downstream stage probability and published lineage.
- **D-11:** Resolve two-leg ties by aggregate goals under the editioned leg-order policy, with no away-goals rule. If level after leg two, apply required extra time and penalties at the second-leg venue. The final is one match at a neutral venue with its official extra-time/penalty policy.
- **D-12:** Completed draw and knockout evidence is authoritative only when it belongs to the same accepted edition/source lineage. Missing, stale, partial, or contradictory draw artifacts produce an explicit unresolved path state.

#### Reproducibility and fail-closed artifacts
- **D-13:** Bind the accepted source bundle, ruleset, club release, accepted draw artifacts, information cutoff, simulation algorithm/version, simulation count, and seed into the projection-run identity and canonical artifact hashes. — **Reversibility:** one-way — changing this published identity contract requires a versioned artifact migration and validator compatibility path.
- **D-14:** Identical accepted inputs and seed must reproduce byte-equivalent canonical outcome artifacts; generation timestamps or filesystem order must not affect identities or row order.
- **D-15:** Per-club probabilities must reconcile with prerequisite league bands, remain monotone from play-off through champion, sum exactly across mutually exclusive outcomes, and be validated before any artifact is accepted. Dashboard-side normalization is forbidden.
- **D-16:** Missing production authority publishes only typed blocked or unresolved diagnostic state. Fixture-mode evidence remains non-promotable, cannot create a production selector, and cannot overwrite accepted incumbent bytes.

## Summary

The supported edition is UEFA Champions League 2026/27. The official regulations describe a 36-club league phase in which each club receives eight single-leg opponents, four at home and four away; the league phase has one ranking and sends ranks 1–8 directly to the round of 16, ranks 9–24 to knockout-phase play-offs, and ranks 25–36 out of the competition. UEFA’s 2026/27 league-phase draw material states that the complete schedule is 144 matches over eight matchdays. [CITED: https://documents.uefa.com/r/Regulations-of-the-UEFA-Champions-League-2026/27/Article-16-Draw-system-league-phase-Online] [CITED: https://documents.uefa.com/r/Regulations-of-the-UEFA-Champions-League-2026/27/Article-17-Match-system-league-phase-Online] [CITED: https://www.uefa.com/uefachampionsleague/news/02a8-215821715a96-9a3b43fad585-1000--uefa-champions-league-league-phase-draw/]

Plan Phase 20 as an editioned UCL rules/state boundary rather than a dashboard feature: validate the accepted Phase 18 graph, canonicalize it through the Phase 14 match-state and standings seams, apply an explicit Article 18 ranking adapter and trace, and publish a sibling outcome bundle modelled on Phase 15. The Phase 19 production club-release resolver is the only production forecast authority. At the current repository state, real UCL authority and the selector-authorized club release are not accepted, so production must remain typed blocked/human-needed while fixture data proves the mechanics only. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

The simulation should be a conditional state transition system: accepted completed results are immutable inputs; only open, eligible fixtures with pre-kickoff approved forecasts are sampled; rank distributions feed legal rank-constrained draw paths until a validated same-edition draw artifact exists; and every knockout resolution carries venue, leg order, aggregate, extra-time, penalty, and draw-policy lineage. The final validator must reject unresolved rank-dependent paths, illegal draw edges, probability reconciliation failures, changed completed forecasts, nondeterministic row order, and fixture-to-production authority escalation. [VERIFIED: codebase grep] [CITED: https://documents.uefa.com/r/Regulations-of-the-UEFA-Champions-League-2026/27/Article-19-Draw-system-knockout-phase-Online]

**Primary recommendation:** Implement one UCL edition contract plus three narrow modules — rules/state, simulation, and outcomes — that reuse Phase 14/18/19 contracts and fail closed whenever accepted source, late tie evidence, draw evidence, or club-release authority is missing. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

### Pattern 1: Editioned Rules Contract and Trace

**What:** Define one data-driven ucl_2026_27 rules object containing ruleset version, source-document IDs/URLs, hash, league cardinality, Article 18 criterion order, interim policy, qualification bands, knockout stages, leg-order policy, tie resolution, final venue policy, and draw-policy identity. Make every ranking and stage resolver accept this object rather than hard-code a generic UEFA rule. [VERIFIED: codebase grep] [CITED: https://documents.uefa.com/r/Regulations-of-the-UEFA-Champions-League-2026/27-Online]

**When to use:** Every rank, band, draw-path, stage-slot, and outcome artifact must carry the edition and ruleset hash. A future UCL edition or a UEFA exception should create a new rules object, not mutate 2026/27 semantics. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**Article 18 final order:** After equal points, the official sequence is (1) superior goal difference, (2) goals scored, (3) away goals scored, (4) wins, (5) away wins, (6) collective points of opponents, (7) collective goal difference of opponents, (8) collective goals scored by opponents, (9) lower disciplinary points over all league-phase matches, and (10) higher club coefficient under Annex D. [CITED: https://documents.uefa.com/r/Regulations-of-the-UEFA-Champions-League-2026/27/Article-18-Equality-of-points-league-phase-Online]

**Interim rule:** UEFA’s standings explainer says that during matchdays 1–7 only the first five Article 18 criteria are used; if clubs remain level they are displayed with the same ranking and ordered alphabetically by abbreviated name for presentation. That display order is not an eligibility rank under D-02/D-09; the project should retain a shared unresolved interval and suppress a path that needs the exact order. [CITED: https://www.uefa.com/uefachampionsleague/news/0291-1bd88ae04870-e1e038c319e3-1000--champions-league-league-phase-standings-how-teams-level-on-/] [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**Trace shape:** A practical trace row should include edition_id, tie_group_id, criterion_order, criterion_id, subset_before, subset_after, evidence_status, source_artifact_ids, decisive, rank_interval_min, rank_interval_max, ruleset_version, and row_sha256. A final table row should reference the decisive trace row or an unresolved interval ID. This mirrors the existing Phase 15 subset/criterion trace pattern. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

### Pattern 2: Accepted Schedule Completeness Before Ranking

**What:** Validate the complete accepted graph before computing standings or forecasts. Require one edition, 36 unique stable club IDs, exactly 144 unique accepted fixtures, no self-fixtures, both endpoints in the club set, eight distinct opponents per club, four home and four away fixtures per club, matchday values for the eight league matchdays, confirmed kickoff, venue/home-away semantics, lifecycle status, score fields, and source artifact/row hashes. [CITED: https://documents.uefa.com/r/Regulations-of-the-UEFA-Champions-League-2026/27/Article-17-Match-system-league-phase-Online] [CITED: https://www.uefa.com/uefachampionsleague/news/02a8-215821715a96-9a3b43fad585-1000--uefa-champions-league-league-phase-draw/] [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

The current Phase 18 football-data adapter emits stable provider match IDs, kickoff, status, stage, matchday, club IDs, full-time scores, and source hashes. Its canonical match projection does not itself establish the eight-opponent/home-away graph or an explicit venue field, so Phase 20 needs a UCL schedule validator/enrichment boundary rather than assuming the provider table is complete. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**Never infer:** Do not complete a partial source by constructing the missing round-robin edges, copying a provider rank, or filling a missing venue/kickoff from a web page. A partial or contradictory graph becomes a typed blocked state with the accepted incumbent retained according to Phase 18. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

~~~r
validate_ucl_schedule <- function(matches, clubs) {
  stopifnot(length(unique(matches$edition_id)) == 1L)
  stopifnot(nrow(clubs) == 36L, nrow(matches) == 144L)
  stopifnot(!any(matches$home_club_id == matches$away_club_id))
  stopifnot(all(c(matches$home_club_id, matches$away_club_id) %in% clubs$club_id))
  degree <- table(c(as.character(matches$home_club_id), as.character(matches$away_club_id)))
  stopifnot(all(as.integer(degree[clubs$club_id]) == 8L))
  stopifnot(all(table(matches$home_club_id) == 4L))
  stopifnot(all(table(matches$away_club_id) == 4L))
  invisible(TRUE)
}
~~~

The final implementation must also validate matchday, venue, lifecycle, score, and lineage fields; the compact example only shows the cardinality invariants. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

### Pattern 3: Immutable Forecast Ledger

**What:** Produce a ledger row for every accepted league fixture, whether forecastable or suppressed. A forecastable row must carry the Phase 19 production release ID, selector/manifest hashes, model/calibrator hashes, feature cutoff strictly earlier than kickoff, calibrated 1X2 probabilities, expected goals, likely score, source bundle, and parent state hashes. A suppressed row carries a closed reason such as release_unavailable, kickoff_unconfirmed, identity_unresolved, insufficient_model_evidence, or cutoff_violation. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**Merge rule:** On refresh, match by immutable fixture identity. If the fixture is completed and a prior pre-kickoff forecast exists, retain the prior bytes and reject any attempted regeneration or replacement. If the fixture is open, a new forecast is allowed only when the release identity and strict cutoff are valid. If no forecast exists, retain the status row; never drop the fixture. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**Authority rule:** Call the Phase 19 production resolver with its fixed trusted root. Do not pass a fixture root, caller-selected release, or national-team release into a production path. Fixture releases may exercise this merge in tests but cannot create a selector or overwrite an incumbent. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

### Pattern 4: Conditional League Simulation

### Performance Strategy and Bounded Simulation

The accepted graph is small and fixed at 36 clubs and 144 league fixtures, so schedule validation and one-state ranking are linear in the accepted fixture rows; the expensive operation is repeated future-outcome and draw-path simulation. [CITED: https://www.uefa.com/uefachampionsleague/news/02a8-215821715a96-9a3b43fad585-1000--uefa-champions-league-league-phase-draw/] [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

Do not enumerate all score outcomes for all open fixtures. Reuse each approved fixture’s compact score grid, sample only eligible open rows, and aggregate rank/band/stage counters incrementally instead of retaining every full simulated tournament state. Keep fixed completed rows outside the sampling loop. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

For pre-draw knockout uncertainty, precompute legal rank-slot transitions and memoize equivalent slot states. Enumerate only when the measured legal state space is bounded; otherwise use a seeded legal sampler whose draw-policy ID, sample count, seed, and legality checks are part of the manifest. Do not claim exact probabilities for an unmeasured or incomplete path space. [VERIFIED: codebase grep] [ASSUMED]

The project instructions identify a 50,000-simulation under-10-second target on M1/M2 Macs for the Monte Carlo component; Phase 20 should include a focused benchmark/diagnostic for the chosen implementation, while full scheduled performance and failure-injection acceptance remains Phase 22. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

### Pattern 5: Legal Rank-Constrained Draw Paths

**League draw boundary:** Phase 20 consumes an accepted eight-opponent schedule; it does not reconstruct the league draw. UEFA’s league draw procedure says software selects valid opponents so the full schedule can complete and the process is externally audited, which reinforces accepting the reviewed schedule artifact rather than implementing a second draw engine. [CITED: https://www.uefa.com/uefachampionsleague/news/02a8-215821715a96-9a3b43fad585-1000--uefa-champions-league-league-phase-draw/]

**Knockout play-off draw:** Article 19 defines seeded pairs 9/10, 11/12, 13/14, and 15/16; unseeded pairs 17/18, 19/20, 21/22, and 23/24; and pair families 9/10 vs 23/24, 11/12 vs 21/22, 13/14 vs 19/20, and 15/16 vs 17/18. Seeded clubs are, in principle, assigned the second-leg home match. [CITED: https://documents.uefa.com/r/Regulations-of-the-UEFA-Champions-League-2026/27/Article-19-Draw-system-knockout-phase-Online]

**Round-of-16 draw:** Article 19 defines seeded pairs 1/2, 3/4, 5/6, and 7/8. Each pair is drawn into one of two bracket positions against the corresponding play-off winner, with the seeded club, in principle, hosting the return leg. The quarter-final and semi-final bracket topology and inherited seed positions come from Annex B; a defeated seed’s successor inherits its bracket position rather than triggering a new ranking. [CITED: https://documents.uefa.com/r/Regulations-of-the-UEFA-Champions-League-2026/27/Article-19-Draw-system-knockout-phase-Online] [CITED: https://documents.uefa.com/r/Regulations-of-the-UEFA-Champions-League-2026/27/Annex-B-UEFA-Champions-League-Competition-System-Online]

**Before a draw artifact:** Represent only pairings allowed by these rank slots and the editioned draw procedure. If unresolved rank intervals overlap a seed boundary, branch only over rank assignments that are actually provable/legal; if an exact rank is required but not provable, mark that path unresolved. Deterministic enumeration is preferable when the legal state space is demonstrably bounded; otherwise use a seeded sampler with a draw-policy hash and legality test on every sampled path. The exact enumeration/sampling choice is discretionary and must be benchmarked/recorded by the plan. [VERIFIED: codebase grep] [ASSUMED]

**After a draw artifact:** Require one accepted, complete, same-edition draw artifact with source lineage, draw ID, rank inputs, pairings, leg order, bracket positions, and content hash. Condition exactly on those pairings; do not sample alternative draws or overwrite them with a generic bracket. Missing, stale, partial, or contradictory draw evidence yields an unresolved path state. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**Edition exceptions:** UEFA’s regulations allow the organiser to alter draw conditions or directly assign positions under stated constraints. The rules contract therefore needs an explicit draw-policy/exception field and cannot assume a permanent generic “same association” rule that is not present in the accepted edition evidence. [CITED: https://documents.uefa.com/r/Regulations-of-the-UEFA-Champions-League-2026/27/Article-16-Draw-system-league-phase-Online] [CITED: https://documents.uefa.com/r/Regulations-of-the-UEFA-Champions-League-2026/27/Article-19-Draw-system-knockout-phase-Online]

### Pattern 6: Editioned Knockout Resolution

**Two legs:** Except for the final, knockout ties are home-and-away two-leg ties and the club with the greater aggregate goals advances. If aggregate scores are level after the second leg, play two 15-minute extra-time periods at the second-leg venue; if still level, resolve by penalties. The current rules do not use away goals as a tiebreak. [CITED: https://documents.uefa.com/r/Regulations-of-the-UEFA-Champions-League-2026/27/Article-20-Match-system-knockout-phase-Online] [CITED: https://documents.uefa.com/r/Regulations-of-the-UEFA-Champions-League-2026/27/Article-21-Knockout-system-extra-time-and-penalty-shoot-outs-Online]

**Leg order:** The play-off and seeded knockout draw rules determine which participant hosts the return leg in principle; carry that accepted leg order into every pair, and reject a pair with two home legs, no home leg, or an unsubstantiated leg-order override. [CITED: https://documents.uefa.com/r/Regulations-of-the-UEFA-Champions-League-2026/27/Article-19-Draw-system-knockout-phase-Online]

**Final:** The final is one match at a predetermined neutral stadium. A tie after normal time proceeds through two 15-minute extra-time periods and then penalties. The silver-side “home” designation is administrative and must not be treated as venue advantage. [CITED: https://documents.uefa.com/r/Regulations-of-the-UEFA-Champions-League-2026/27/Article-22-Match-system-final-Online]

Reuse and adapt the tested Phase 15 primitives uefa_nl_validate_two_leg_pair, uefa_nl_resolve_two_leg_tie, and uefa_nl_resolve_single_leg behind the UCL rules adapter. Do not copy Nations League assumptions such as its lower-league play-off rule or its group/head-to-head tiebreak sequence. [VERIFIED: codebase grep] [CITED: https://documents.uefa.com/r/Regulations-of-the-UEFA-Champions-League-2026/27/Article-18-Equality-of-points-league-phase-Online]

## Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: Provider rank silently becomes project rank

**What goes wrong:** A provider’s position or band is displayed as the official project answer even when the project cannot reproduce the tie-break or provider evidence is from another source bundle. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**Why it happens:** The Phase 18 source contains a standings table and it is tempting to treat it as authoritative. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**How to avoid:** Compute arithmetic from accepted match state, run the UCL Article 18 adapter, and retain provider metrics only in reconciliation evidence. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**Warning signs:** A standings row has no ruleset hash/trace ID, or a provider rank changes an eligibility band without a reconciliation status. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

### Pitfall 2: Interim display order is used as final qualification order

**What goes wrong:** Alphabetical abbreviated club names during matchdays 1–7 are used to decide a rank-8 or rank-24 boundary. [CITED: https://www.uefa.com/uefachampionsleague/news/0291-1bd88ae04870-e1e038c319e3-1000--champions-league-league-phase-standings-how-teams-level-on-/]

**Why it happens:** The standings page displays a total order even while late criteria are not available. [CITED: https://www.uefa.com/uefachampionsleague/news/0291-1bd88ae04870-e1e038c319e3-1000--champions-league-league-phase-standings-how-teams-level-on-/]

**How to avoid:** Preserve the equal-rank interval and mark band/path probabilities unresolved if the interval crosses rank 8 or 24. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**Warning signs:** A lexical comparison appears in a rules adapter or a probability row exists for a rank-dependent path while the tie trace says evidence unavailable. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

### Pitfall 3: Generic UEFA tiebreaks are copied from Nations League

**What goes wrong:** Head-to-head criteria or group-specific recursion from the Nations League adapter are applied to the UCL league phase. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**Why it happens:** Phase 15 is the nearest implementation analog, but its competition rules differ. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**How to avoid:** Encode only Article 18’s UCL sequence and test each criterion order plus the interim first-five policy. [CITED: https://documents.uefa.com/r/Regulations-of-the-UEFA-Champions-League-2026/27/Article-18-Equality-of-points-league-phase-Online]

**Warning signs:** A UCL rules object contains head_to_head_points, or a trace skips opponent-collective/discipline/coefficient evidence. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

### Pitfall 4: Incomplete schedule is made complete by inference

**What goes wrong:** Missing pairings, venues, or kickoff times are generated from a round-robin formula or copied from an unrelated page. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**Why it happens:** The expected 144-edge graph is known, so a developer may fill gaps to make cardinality pass. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**How to avoid:** Require accepted source rows and hashes for all 144 fixtures; fail closed on missing or contradictory edges. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**Warning signs:** Synthetic IDs, generated matchdays, or a source-lineage field that points to a generated row rather than an accepted artifact. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

### Pitfall 5: Completed forecasts are regenerated after results arrive

**What goes wrong:** A later run sees a completed match and recomputes the forecast using information unavailable before kickoff, destroying historical auditability. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**Why it happens:** The generic forecast builder is batch-oriented and can generate a fresh row for the current match state. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**How to avoid:** Merge by immutable fixture ID and retain the prior pre-kickoff row byte-for-byte once a fixture is completed; only an open fixture can receive a replacement. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**Warning signs:** generated_at_utc is newer than kickoff for a completed forecast, or a completed row’s model/release/cutoff hash changes between runs. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

### Pitfall 6: Completed results are resampled

**What goes wrong:** A simulation samples every fixture from a forecast grid, changing accepted results and making “conditioned on current state” meaningless. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**Why it happens:** A simple loop does not separate lifecycle status before sampling. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**How to avoid:** Partition fixed and eligible-open fixtures once per iteration; only call the score sampler for the eligible set. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**Warning signs:** A completed fixture has a random draw seed or appears in sampled-future metadata. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

### Pitfall 7: Illegal knockout paths are assigned probability

**What goes wrong:** Teams are paired uniformly, a fixed bracket is assumed, or a rank-interval tie is resolved randomly before the draw. [CITED: https://documents.uefa.com/r/Regulations-of-the-UEFA-Champions-League-2026/27/Article-19-Draw-system-knockout-phase-Online]

**Why it happens:** Uniform pairing is easier to code than rank-pair families and bracket positions. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**How to avoid:** Generate only policy-validated rank paths; after an accepted draw, use the exact artifact and do not branch. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**Warning signs:** A draw policy is absent from simulation metadata, pairings ignore 9/10-vs-23/24 families, or a draw artifact hash is present but its pairs are not used. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

### Pitfall 8: Away-goals or wrong leg venue is reintroduced

**What goes wrong:** A tie is decided by away goals, extra time is applied at the wrong venue, or a seeded club is assigned the return leg contrary to the accepted draw policy. [CITED: https://documents.uefa.com/r/Regulations-of-the-UEFA-Champions-League-2026/27/Article-21-Knockout-system-extra-time-and-penalty-shoot-outs-Online]

**Why it happens:** Older competition implementations or generic two-leg helpers may carry assumptions not valid for this edition. [ASSUMED]

**How to avoid:** Store both leg rows, aggregate regulation/final goals, resolve extra time and penalties only after leg two at its venue, and test the no-away-goals case explicitly. [CITED: https://documents.uefa.com/r/Regulations-of-the-UEFA-Champions-League-2026/27/Article-20-Match-system-knockout-phase-Online]

**Warning signs:** A resolver reads away-goal totals, or a single-leg final is represented as two home/away rows. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

### Pitfall 9: Fixture mechanics become production authority

**What goes wrong:** A synthetic fixture bundle or model release is written to a production selector or accepted current-state path. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**Why it happens:** Fixture data is complete enough to make positive tests pass. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**How to avoid:** Keep fixture roots process-temporary, mark them non-promotable, resolve production paths internally, and test protected production bytes. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**Warning signs:** A production CLI accepts a fixture-root flag, or a fixture authority row has promotion_eligible=TRUE. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

### Pitfall 10: Replay hash changes for irrelevant metadata

**What goes wrong:** Repeated runs differ because timestamps, filesystem traversal order, or unsorted IDs enter canonical bytes. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**Why it happens:** R data frames preserve input order and manifests often include generated time. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**How to avoid:** Sort every canonical table by semantic key, exclude generation timestamps from identity payloads where the contract says so, and hash the same canonical representation on read and write. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**Warning signs:** Reverse-input tests fail, a new temp directory changes the manifest hash, or only generated_at_utc differs in a supposedly byte-equivalent output. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]
